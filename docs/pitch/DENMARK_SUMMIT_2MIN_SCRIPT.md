# Mframapa 2 minute pitch (Frances)

Before I start, take a breath with me.

You just breathed. Can you tell me what was in that air?

Most of us can’t. Across much of Africa, there often isn’t a monitor close enough to say. Parents still decide whether children go outside without knowing how clean or dirty the air is.

mframapa gives people a daily estimate of air quality for African cities. How clean or dirty the air is today, what that means for your health, in a language you understand. Free in your browser at mframapa.live.

We built it in Ghana. We look up.

It works because the atmosphere is already watched from space every day. Satellites and weather see dust, smoke, and the conditions that trap or clear dirty air across whole regions. Our AI trains on real ground stations, then produces a daily city estimate people can use. The app turns that into plain guidance: go outside, take it easy, or be careful.

Try Accra, Lagos, Nairobi, Cape Town, and more cities across Africa.

It is built for people first: families, schools, clinics, anyone planning their day. 27 languages widely spoken across Africa, from French, to Portuguese in Cabo Verde, to isiZulu in South Africa. Phones first. Slow connections welcome. Your last reading stays on the phone when data drops.

These are estimates from space, made useful on the ground. You cannot protect what you cannot see.

I’m Frances, from the mframapa team in Accra. Open mframapa.live, and if this belongs in your work, find me after.

Thank you.
